# Tape symbols

Each instruction on a tape is one token pressed into the brass strip, in the Fired Workshop material language of art/BIBLE.md. A token fuses the key that writes it with the action it performs: the letter is the form the action is done on or with, never a letter beside a pictogram. Tokens never turn on the tape, so every one carries the same soft highlight from the upper left. They are generated by art/textures/gen.sh; every prompt is on record in art/symbols/prompts.txt, the pick in art/symbols/<key>.png, the rest under art/symbols/candidates/, all of them on art/symbols/sheet.png (art/sheet.sh). art/symbols/proof.sh renders art/symbols/proof/tape-26px.png, the strip at 1:1 above a 4x enlargement, from the `focus` shot scene, whose tape holds all seven.

## Two glazes per token

A token is drawn at 26 px on the strip, and at that size a brass letter on brass is a smudge: the first round's A, D and X were one bronze and their outlines dissolved. So every token is two glazes from the look table (src/look.rs `Glaze`, declared per key as `Token { face, field }` in `KEYS`): the letter's face and the ground it sits on, far enough apart in value that the edge survives the downsample. Measured at 26 px, no dark glaze reaches that gap against the brass strip, so terracotta, blue-green and plum sit as letters on a light ivory tile, amber and ivory letters sit on dark brass, and the two mirror pairs flip figure and ground. The gate is src/look.rs: at 26 px every face and field wears its declared glaze, every letter is at least VALUE_APART from its field, and every pair of tokens differs in hue or value on the face or on the field, all under the one set of thresholds the rest of the look table uses. The first round's tokens are kept under candidates/round1/ as the set that fails it. What survives to 26 px is the letter and its two colours; the hands, beads and arrows below are the token's story at full size on the sheet, not what the tape shows.

## F, grab: the hand is the letter

A closed hand in terracotta glaze, the colour of every closed hand on the board, curls into a capital F on an ivory tile: the stem is the wrist, the two bars are two fingers closing on a blue-green bead in the crook. The letter is not held by the hand; the hand is the letter, and the bead names what a grab closes on.

## R, drop: the hand is the bowl

A blue-green capital R on an ivory tile whose bowl is no stroke at all but an open hand in the same glaze, palm out, fingers curled into the curve the bowl would have made, with a blue-green bead dropped free below it. The hand is a piece of the letter, not a passenger in it; the R only closes because the hand is open. Grab and drop share the hand, the bead and the tile, so the pair reads as one verb and its undo: terracotta closed, blue-green open.

## A, counterclockwise: the letter is the arm on its pivot

An amber capital A on dark brass, hung from a round brass pivot pin at its apex and swung counterclockwise, leaning left, with an amber arrowhead in the corner it swings toward. The arm on the board is brass on a pivot and turns about it; the A is that arm, pinned where the arm is pinned, caught mid-turn in the direction the key turns it.

## D, clockwise: the same pivot, the other way

An amber tile with a capital D cut through it to the brass beneath, hung from a round brass pivot pin at the top of its stem and swung clockwise, leaning right, its bowl bulging into the turn, with a cut arc behind it whose head points the way it went. A and D are the one action in two directions, so both hang a letter from a pivot pin and lean into the sweep, mirrored; the figure and ground flip, amber on brass against brass in amber, is what tells them apart at tape size.

## Q, pivot counterclockwise: the hand is the pin

A plum capital Q on an ivory tile gripped by a small terracotta horseshoe, the open hand the board draws, and turned counterclockwise about it, leaning left, with a plum arrowhead in the corner it swings toward. A pivot turns the molecule about the hand while the arm stays, so where A and D hang their letter from the arm's pivot pin, Q and E hold theirs by the hand: the same lean as the rotates, but the fixed point is a hand, not a pin.

## E, pivot clockwise: the same hand, the other way

An ivory capital E on a plum tile gripped at the tip of its middle bar by the same terracotta horseshoe and turned clockwise about it, leaning right, with an ivory arrowhead in the corner it swings toward. Q and E are one action in two directions, as A and D are, and flip figure and ground the same way: plum on ivory, ivory on plum. Plum against amber is what tells the pivots from the rotates at tape size; the hand at the centre tells them apart at full size.

## X, wait: a mark painted on the bare strip

A capital X in thick ivory glaze fired straight onto dark brass, no ceramic tile under it. Wait adds nothing to the board, so its mark adds nothing to the tape but a stroke of the product's own white where a step was struck out.
