# Tape symbols

Each instruction on a tape is one token pressed into the brass strip, in the Fired Workshop material language of art/BIBLE.md. A token fuses the key that writes it with the action it performs: the letter is the form the action is done on or with, never a letter beside a pictogram. Tokens never turn on the tape, so every one carries the same soft highlight from the upper left. They are generated by art/textures/gen.sh; every prompt is on record in art/symbols/prompts.txt, the pick in art/symbols/<key>.png, the rest under art/symbols/candidates/, all of them on art/symbols/sheet.png (art/sheet.sh). The manual page held up on Tab (art/overlay/) is generated over the thirteen picks, so a repainted symbol makes it stale; `art/overlay/gen.sh -c` says so. art/symbols/proof.sh renders the strip at 1:1 above a 4x enlargement from a shot scene, by default art/symbols/proof/tape-26px.png from the `focus` scene, whose tape holds all thirteen; art/symbols/proof/moves-26px.png is the `walk` scene's strip, the six move tokens running with a stall mark.

## Two glazes per token

A token is drawn at 26 px on the strip, and at that size a brass letter on brass is a smudge: the first round's A, D and X were one bronze and their outlines dissolved. So every token is two glazes from the look table (src/look.rs `Glaze`, declared per key as `Token { face, field }` in `KEYS`): the letter's face and the ground it sits on, far enough apart in value that the edge survives the downsample. Measured at 26 px, no dark glaze reaches that gap against the brass strip, so among the first seven terracotta, blue-green and plum sit as letters on a light ivory tile, amber and ivory letters sit on dark brass, and the two mirror pairs flip figure and ground. The gate is src/look.rs: at 26 px every face and field wears its declared glaze, every letter is at least VALUE_APART from its field, and every pair of tokens differs in hue or value on the face or on the field, all under the one set of thresholds the rest of the look table uses. The first round's tokens are kept under candidates/round1/ as the set that fails it. What survives to 26 px is the letter and its two colours; the hands, beads and arrows below are the token's story at full size on the sheet, not what the tape shows. Two glazes are alike to the gate when neither hue nor value tells them apart: brass and terracotta, clay and amber. With the seven placed, the pairs left for the six moves are amber on ivory and ivory on amber, blue-green on clay and clay on blue-green, plum on clay and clay on plum, and each opposite pair of moves takes a mirrored pair. Clay is the board's glaze and no token wore it before; four of the moves wear it, as letter or as tile, since a move is the arm crossing the board.

## F, grab: the hand is the letter

A closed hand in terracotta glaze, the colour of every closed hand on the board, curls into a capital F on an ivory tile: the stem is the wrist, the two bars are two fingers closing on a blue-green bead in the crook. The letter is not held by the hand; the hand is the letter, and the bead names what a grab closes on.

## R, drop: the hand is the bowl

A blue-green capital R on an ivory tile whose bowl is no stroke at all but an open hand in the same glaze, palm out, fingers curled into the curve the bowl would have made, with a blue-green bead dropped free below it. The hand is a piece of the letter, not a passenger in it; the R only closes because the hand is open. Grab and drop share the hand, the bead and the tile, so the pair reads as one verb and its undo: terracotta closed, blue-green open.

## A, counterclockwise: the letter is the arm on its pivot

An amber capital A on dark brass, hung from a round brass pivot pin at its apex and swung counterclockwise, leaning left, with an amber arrowhead in the corner it swings toward. The arm on the board is brass on a pivot and turns about it; the A is that arm, pinned where the arm is pinned, caught mid-turn in the direction the key turns it.

## D, clockwise: the same pivot, the other way

An amber tile with a capital D cut through it to the brass beneath, hung from a round brass pivot pin at the top of its stem and swung clockwise, leaning right, its bowl bulging into the turn, with a cut arc behind it whose head points the way it went. A and D are the one action in two directions, so both hang a letter from a pivot pin and lean into the sweep, mirrored; the figure and ground flip, amber on brass against brass in amber, is what tells them apart at tape size.

## Q, pivot counterclockwise: the hand is the pin

A plum capital Q on an ivory tile gripped by a small terracotta horseshoe, the open hand the board draws, and turned counterclockwise about it, leaning left, with a plum arrowhead in the corner it swings toward. A pivot turns the molecule about the hand while the arm stays, so where A and D hang their letter from the arm's pivot pin, Q and E hold theirs by the hand: the same lean as the rotates, but the fixed point is a hand, not a pin.

## E, pivot clockwise: the same hand, the other way

An ivory capital E on a plum tile gripped at the tip of its middle bar by the same terracotta horseshoe and turned clockwise about it, leaning right, with an ivory arrowhead in the corner it swings toward. Q and E are one action in two directions, as A and D are, and flip figure and ground the same way: plum on ivory, ivory on plum. Plum against amber is what tells the pivots from the rotates at tape size; the hand at the centre tells them apart at full size.

## X, wait: a mark painted on the bare strip

A capital X in thick ivory glaze fired straight onto dark brass, no ceramic tile under it. Wait adds nothing to the board, so its mark adds nothing to the tape but a stroke of the product's own white where a step was struck out.

## Shift W, move upper left: the letter slides on its pin

An amber capital W on an ivory tile, hung from a small round brass pivot pin at the top of its middle peak as the A hangs from its apex, but slid, not swung: pin and letter have moved toward the upper left along a short groove the pin cut in the tile behind them, with an amber arrowhead in the corner they slide toward. The arm's base on the board is that brass pin, a move is the pin sliding one cell, and the letter is the arm it carries. The six moves are one family, told apart at full size by the way the pin has slid and at tape size by their glazes, since the letter's edge is all that survives there.

## Shift E, move upper right: the same slide, pinned at the bar

A blue-green capital E on a clay tile, pinned at the tip of its middle bar where the pivot E is gripped by a hand, and slid toward the upper right along its groove, a blue-green arrowhead in that corner. A pin, not a hand: the pivot turns the molecule about the hand and the move carries the whole arm by its pin.

## Shift F, move right: the same slide, pinned at the bar's end

A plum capital F on a clay tile, pinned at the end of its top bar and slid toward the right, its groove trailing left and a plum arrowhead at the right edge. F and A are opposites and flip figure and ground as A and D do: plum on clay against clay on plum.

## Shift C, move lower right: the same slide on amber

An ivory capital C on an amber tile, pinned at its upper tip and slid toward the lower right, the groove trailing to the upper left and an ivory arrowhead in the lower right corner. W and C are opposites: amber on ivory against ivory on amber.

## Shift X, move lower left: the same slide on blue-green

A clay capital X on a blue-green tile, pinned at its crossing and slid toward the lower left, the groove trailing to the upper right and a clay arrowhead in the lower left corner. E and X are opposites: blue-green on clay against clay on blue-green. The wait X is ivory on bare brass; this one is a clay letter on a glazed tile, and the tile is what tells them apart at tape size.

## Shift A, move left: the same slide on plum

A clay capital A on a plum tile, pinned at its apex as the rotate A is, and slid toward the left, the groove trailing right and a clay arrowhead at the left edge. The rotate A swings on its pin and stays amber on brass; this one slides on it and is clay on plum.
