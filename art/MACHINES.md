# Machine bible — Fired Workshop

The machines are compact kiln-floor tools standing on the board rather than symbols painted on wells or plates: a sprite carries only the machine, and the board's own tiles show around it. Each is a top-down ceramic-and-brass object with a simple silhouette, one readable material story, and empty atom seats left unobstructed. Fine valves, collars, channels, and service plates reward a close look; broad seats and channels carry meaning at board scale.

Every seat is painted at the exact centre of its cell, the arm's pivot and hand included: the sim puts an atom at the centre of its cell and a seat is where the machine meets that atom, so an off-centre seat shows the bead beside its cup, and an arm turns about its pivot cell's centre. The pipeline holds the rule: the scaffold marks each centre, every capture is registered by its seats, and the score rejects one whose seats still miss their centres by more than the manifest's `off_centre` share of a cell's circumradius.

Every machine is drawn from directly above (BIBLE.md, View): the shared prompt says so, and the critic refuses a capture that shows a side face or a cup's far wall, since the code score has no honest measure of it. The critic is the keep's eye: `critic.sh` shows the image tool each candidate the code passes as the game places it, unlit, the cut sprite on the board at the shipped size, beside its scaffold, with the manifest's `critic` rubric, the bible's View, Footprint, Language, Silhouette, Glyphs and Forbidden quoted, and reads back one score, 0 to 10, and a ranked list of issues; the score is one more column of `scores.tsv` against the manifest's `critic` threshold, and a candidate under it fails the keep like any rule. The rubric and the threshold are a rule, not a dial (AGENTS.md).

Every machine stays within its tiles (BIBLE.md, Footprint): no pixel the key leaves visible lies beyond the union of its footprint hexes by more than the manifest's `outside` share of a circumradius, and the score reports the farthest one.

## How a machine texture is made

The second round receives the best candidate's issues; the third drops that list and starts again from the machine description, prioritizing the whole gameplay read and seat layout before detail.

The game owns geometry and judgement; the generator only paints. Each machine's entry in `machines/manifest.toml` carries its art direction under one of two keys, and exactly one: `placeholder`, direction the pipeline or a programmer wrote to have something to paint from, or `given`, direction a person wrote to be used as it stands. The text under either is the machine's own prompt; the shared style text is prepended at generation and never merged into it, and given text is never rewritten, reflowed or corrected. `cargo run -- --plan` lists every machine with the kind of direction it carries and whether a recipe feeds it, and counts the placeholders, so what is still programmer art is one command away. One command remakes machines, run from the repo root inside `nix-shell`: `cargo run -- --gen NAME...` remakes the named machines and `--gen --all` every machine. It is idempotent: a machine's candidates are keyed by a hash of its prompt, the candidate count, its scaffold pixels and its recipe (the manifest's `painted`), its maps by a hash of the kept candidate's bytes, the relight prompt and the edges (`relit`), and its critic scores by a hash of the critic rubric and every candidate's bytes (`judged`); a machine whose keys match and whose files exist is skipped past a re-score of its kept candidate, a changed prompt paints its candidates again, a changed rubric voids every critic score, each candidate judged again when it is next looked at, and an unchanged candidate is never judged twice, and a hand-picked candidate is one edit to `kept` followed by the same command, which redoes only the cut and the relief. The measured rules remain hard. The critic's 8 is the target: reaching it ends the rounds early; otherwise the best measured-rule candidate after three rounds is kept with its critic score and issues in `scores.tsv`. Candidates and relights of every machine in the run paint concurrently, at most six at once; `paint.sh` retries the image tool with doubling backoff, four attempts, each failure on stderr; every candidate scored prints one line with its numbers and any measured rule it fails; `sheet.png` is remade only when a candidate was painted or judged or it is missing; the exit code is non-zero only when a requested machine did not land, with the reason; a sheet that fails to draw is reported and fails nothing. A bible tweak is one edit to the manifest and one command.

1. **Scaffold.** The scaffold is drawn from the machine's seat marks from the one glyph table at 256 px per hex, turn 0, on a flat pure green chroma key: each seat marked by its role from that table (a blue-green ring for a seat that keeps its atom, a terracotta ring for one that consumes it, a brass disc for the arm's pivot, an open terracotta horseshoe for its hand), a dot of the same glaze at the centre of every ring and horseshoe, inside a green band that the sprite never shows. Nothing marks the footprint itself: any drawn cell, well or outline, a hairline in a darker shade of the key included, comes back as a plate under the machine, a second set of tiles over the board's own. The pixel rectangle is the same `quad` the renderer places the sprite on, so alignment is by construction.
2. **Recipe.** A machine that has a recipe (`sim::RECIPES`) gets its compound drawn by the one renderer that draws the hover card, beads and bars at the scaffold's scale on the card's brass, into `NAME/recipe.png`, kept beside the scaffold. It is drawn when the machine's key changes or the file is missing, so a changed recipe repaints the machine and a changed bead texture does not. `paint.sh` takes any number of images, `-i` each, and hands them all to the image tool with the prompt; a candidate is painted over the scaffold first and the recipe second, and the manifest's `recipe` sentence, added to the shared prompt for a machine that has one, names the image of beads and bars as the inspiration for the machine's form and forbids painting a bead or a bar. The render is framed by the first tier's footprint, the bound every recipe lies within, so a recipe larger than its machine is never clipped. A machine with no recipe is painted over the scaffold alone, as before.
3. **Paint.** The image tool edits the scaffold in place, `candidates` times, with the manifest's shared prompt, the recipe sentence when there is a recipe, and the machine's own direction; the candidates stay under `NAME/candidates/`. `paint.sh` repaints a non-square return rather than forcing it to fit: the sprite goes on a square quad, so a forced fit would squash the art.
4. **Register.** Each painted seat is found as the point about which its rim is most nearly concentric, and the seats are fitted onto their cells by one shift and one uniform scale, never a stretch or a turn, so the tool's drift and its habit of drawing the seats closer together or further apart than the marks cost nothing; what the fit cannot absorb is what the tolerance judges, and a seat beyond half a cell from its mark is unmeasured, so the candidate fails. The score, the cut and the relief all read the registered capture.
5. **Score.** Each candidate is measured by code: `outside`, the farthest any pixel the key leaves visible lies beyond the union of the footprint's hexes, in circumradii; `seat`, the least contrast between any seat disc and the ring around it; `palette`, the distance from the sprite's mean colour to the nearest bible glaze; `off_centre`, how far the seats miss their cells once registered, in circumradii. Each candidate that passes those is then judged by eye: the code composites its cut sprite over the board's tiles at the shipped size, magnified without smoothing, into `NAME/judged/` (not kept in the repo), and `critic.sh` hands that picture and the scaffold to the image tool with the manifest's `critic` rubric, asking for one JSON object, a `score` from 0 to 10 and ranked `issues`, at most five, each one sentence naming what and where; `critic`, the score, joins the columns with the issues beside it, and a reply that is not one such object fails that candidate alone. The thresholds are the manifest's; the manifest's `kept` candidate stays kept while it passes, else the best passing one is kept and its index recorded in the manifest, and `NAME/scores.tsv` keeps every number. The contact sheet `sheet.png` shows every candidate with its scores and the survivor.
6. **Cut.** The kept candidate is keyed by its green into `NAME/albedo.png`, cropped to the quad, the flat base colour with baked ambient occlusion: every pixel the machine does not cover has alpha 0, so the board's tiles show through around it. The footprint judges a candidate; it never cuts one, so a machine ends at its own silhouette.
7. **Relief.** The kept candidate gets a matte grey sphere composited into the band (`NAME/relit/master.png`, not kept in the repo) and is relit by the image tool once per edge the manifest names. The code reads each light's true direction and ambient share off the sphere, solves every pixel's normal by least squares, and writes `NAME/normal.png`; the sphere must come back as a sphere within the manifest's `sphere` degrees, else the four edits are painted again, three sets at most, before the run fails. `relit/lights.txt` records the recovered lights.

At runtime the sprite is a mesh with a tangent, drawn by `Lit`: albedo times a Lambert term under one fixed world light, with the normal rotated by the sprite's turn in the shader, so a rotated machine keeps its lit side on the world's light side and the arm's swing sweeps its highlight continuously.

## Arm

A broad brass pivot, one-piece link, and terracotta rubber-lined horseshoe make a durable manipulator whose wear belongs only at joints and contact edges. Bushings, grease ports, and service plates imply maintenance without exposed clockwork.

## Source

A single blue-green ceramic feed hopper with one unmistakable seat, a brass metering ring, a short delivery chute, and a worn rubber gate. It reads as dispensing one bead, never storing a second.

## Bonder

Two adjacent cells as equal seats joined by a heavy compression bridge. Opposed clamps and shared pressure plumbing say that both atoms survive and leave joined; there is no third or sacrificial intake.

## Second bond

A triangular plum manifold with two matching bond seats and one different sacrificial feed. Paired rails connect the bond seats while the feed uses a funnel, one-way valve, and more severe collar, making the fixed roles legible before an atom arrives.

## Output, first tier

A seven-cell ivory receiving station on the centred hexagon of radius one: seven protected cups, one at the centre and six around it, paired rails from the centre to each, rubber bumpers and one covered exit chute. It looks receptive rather than transformative.

## Output, second tier

The first tier at its heart, ringed by twelve more cups tied on by the same rails and a thin brass ring: nineteen cells, radius two.

## Output, third tier

The second tier at its heart, ringed by eighteen more cups and a second brass ring: thirty-seven cells, radius three.
